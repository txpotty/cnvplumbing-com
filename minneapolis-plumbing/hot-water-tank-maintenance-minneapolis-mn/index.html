    Gas Venting Tune-Up in Minneapolis, MN

    Atmospheric gas tanks vent by draft alone, so the vent connector needs upward slope of at least a quarter inch per foot and tight joints with three screws each. Draft at the hood gets checked first. Lint on the flame arrestor of an FVIR tank can starve the burner and kill the pilot. We vacuum it clean.

    Sediment Flush and Descaling in Minneapolis

    Calcium carbonate drops out of hard water every time the tank heats, and over a year it can harden into chunks the size of a thumbnail. Those chunks cause the popping. When a plain flush will not move them, we use a descaling solution rated for potable systems, let it sit and then flush it all out. A clean bottom puts less heat stress on the seam.

    T&P Valve Replacement in Minneapolis

    Replacing a relief valve is a short job, but it has to be done right. The new valve must match the tank's BTU or kilowatt rating. We drain the tank below the valve opening, thread in the new one with pipe dope rated for potable water, and reconnect a discharge pipe in copper, CPVC or another approved material. A capped discharge pipe is a serious hazard.

    Gas and Electric Tank Checks in Minneapolis, MN

    Gas and electric tanks share the same steel shell, but the parts that wear out are different. On gas units we inspect the burner, thermocouple and vent connector. Electric units get a meter test on both elements and a look at the wiring at each thermostat, where loose terminals can scorch and melt the insulation. Both get a flush.

    Anode Upgrades for Odor in Minneapolis, MN

    A rotten egg smell from the hot side only is almost always sulfate-reducing bacteria reacting with a magnesium anode. A different rod usually solves it. We replace it with an aluminum-zinc rod or a powered anode, then chlorinate the tank to kill the bacteria living in it. Powered anodes never wear out and draw very little electricity.

    Anode Rod Inspection and Replacement in Minneapolis, MN

    The anode rod is a sacrificial metal rod that corrodes so the steel tank does not. Private wells are widespread, so homeowners around Minneapolis should test for hardness and iron before choosing a treatment system. We loosen the hex head with a breaker bar and an impact gun, pull the rod and look at how much core wire is showing. If six inches of wire is bare, it is time for a new one.
