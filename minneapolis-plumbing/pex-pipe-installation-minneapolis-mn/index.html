    PEX-A, PEX-B, and PEX-C Systems in Minneapolis, MN

    There are three grades of PEX, named for how the plastic is cross-linked at the factory. PEX-A is the most flexible and joins with expansion rings, PEX-B is stiffer and joins with crimp rings or cinch clamps, and PEX-C sits closer to B in feel. All three are code-listed for drinking water. Pipe, fittings and tools come from the same maker on our jobs, which keeps the warranty valid.

    Radiant floor loops are PEX too, though not the same pipe you drink from.     PEX-A vs. PEX-B Installation in Minneapolis

    Homeowners ask us which one is better, and the honest answer depends on the job. PEX-A bends tighter, recovers from a kink with a heat gun, and its expansion fittings flow more water, while PEX-B costs less and its crimp joints are just as reliable when they are gauged properly. Either one lasts for decades. In a cramped attic, we usually pick PEX-A.

    PEX rarely fails on its own. Most failures trace back to a nail or screw through the pipe, a rodent working on an attic run, or a fitting that was not crimped fully.     Manifold and Home-Run PEX Layouts in Minneapolis, MN

    Think of a manifold like an electrical panel, but for water lines. A central header feeds each fixture on its own line with its own valve, which lets you shut off one toilet and leave the kitchen running. It uses more pipe. The trade-off is almost no fittings hidden in the walls and steadier pressure when two showers run at once.

    Uponor PEX System Installation in Minneapolis

    Uponor's ProPEX system is PEX-A joined with an expansion tool plus a ring slipped over the pipe end. The tech expands the pipe and ring together, pushes the fitting in, and the plastic shrinks back around the barb over the next few seconds, tighter than when it started. No crimp tool to calibrate. Because the fittings are wide inside, each joint costs very little flow.
