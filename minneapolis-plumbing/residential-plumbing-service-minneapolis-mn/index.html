    Frequently Asked Questions About Residential Plumbing Service in Minneapolis

    Most minor repairs run $150 to $450. Replacing a water heater runs $1,400 to $3,000, depending on size and whether it is gas or electric. You approve a flat price before any work begins, so the bill at the end matches what you agreed to.

    Moen, Delta, Kohler, American Standard, Pfister and TOTO make up most of our installs. We can also install a fixture you already bought, as long as it is a complete unit with the right connections, and we will check the box before the appointment so nothing is missing when we arrive. Bring the receipt.

    Start at the water meter. Shut off every tap and appliance, then watch the small leak dial, and if it still spins, look for warm spots on the floor, cracks in tile, mildew smell or a water bill that jumped without explanation. Then call for a leak test.

    A slab leak is a break in a water line running under a concrete foundation. Glacial clay and sandy soils are both common in Minnesota, and deep frost causes the ground to heave and settle each year. The usual signs are a warm patch of floor, a water bill that jumps or water running when nothing is on. Do not wait on it.

    Two leaks in a year is the tipping point for most homes. Polybutylene and galvanized pipe also tilt the answer toward a repipe, since patching one spot on a failing system just moves the next leak a few feet down the line. You get both prices side by side.

    One to two hours covers many repairs. Plan on half a day for a water heater swap, and a whole-house repipe runs a few days, with the water turned back on each evening so the family can shower and cook. We tell you up front.

    Soft water and normal pressure prevent a lot of what we see. Grease out of the drains and a yearly pressure test help too, since anything over 80 psi wears out supply hoses and fixtures faster than people expect. A yearly inspection catches the rest.

    Once a year is a good rule. Hard water speeds up sediment buildup, so homes with scale may need a flush more often, and a yearly check lets us test the relief valve, look at the anode rod and catch a weeping fitting before it rusts through the jacket. An anode rod often wears out in three to five years.
