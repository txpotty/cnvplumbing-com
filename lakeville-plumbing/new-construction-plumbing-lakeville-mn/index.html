    Frequently Asked Questions About New Construction Plumbing in Lakeville

    Most new single-family homes in the Lakeville area cost between $12,000 and $25,000 to plumb, with larger custom homes running higher. A bathroom adds a few thousand on its own. Tankless heaters and gas runs add to it. Our bid is fixed per phase, so the number you sign is the number you pay unless the plans change.

    Most jurisdictions inspect plumbing three times. First comes underground, with drains holding a water or air test before the pour. The rough or top-out inspection happens before insulation and drywall, and the final comes once fixtures are set, the water heater is running and the gas is tested. Some add a gas test.

    Scheduling causes most of the trouble. A late pour or a framing change can stack plumbing phases on top of each other. Plan changes after rough-in are the other big one, because moving a tub or adding a bar sink once walls are framed means re-drilling and new inspections. We price changes in writing first.

    We install Moen, Kohler, Delta and American Standard, plus most other major brands a builder or designer specifies. Homeowner-supplied fixtures are fine. We ask that they arrive on site before trim day so we can open the boxes and check for missing parts, because a missing cartridge or wrong finish can push trim back a week.

    Underground plumbing on a typical house takes one to three days. Top-out takes three to five days, and final trim takes one or two. Calendar time stretches over months since each phase waits on another trade to finish. Weather moves it too.

    The term is written into your contract before work starts. It covers our labor and the fittings we supply. Manufacturer warranties cover fixtures and heaters, and we register the water heater in your name at final.

    Workmanship is covered under our written warranty, and the length is spelled out in your contract. Fixtures carry their own manufacturer warranties. Water heaters and PEX systems often carry 10 years or more, but only when installed with the matching tools and fittings, which is one reason we do not mix brands.

    For supply, we use PEX-A or PEX-B in most homes, sometimes with a copper main. Drains are PVC or ABS. Some builders want cast iron in walls beside bedrooms, since it is far quieter than PVC when an upstairs toilet flushes.
