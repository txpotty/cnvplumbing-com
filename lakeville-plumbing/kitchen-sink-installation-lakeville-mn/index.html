    How We Install Kitchen Sinks in Lakeville, MN: Style by Style

    Composite Sink Installation in Lakeville

    Granite and quartz composite sinks resist scratches on the surface but can chip at the drain hole if a strainer is overtightened. We tighten by hand first. Composite makers usually call for silicone instead of plumber's putty under the strainer, since some putties stain the material, and we follow that sheet.

    Undermount Sink Installation in Lakeville, MN

    Undermounts hang from clips anchored into the underside of the stone, plus a bead of silicone along the rim. We support the bowl from below with a brace while the silicone cures, usually overnight, before loading it with water. Skipping the cure time leads to sagging. A sink that drops even an eighth of an inch opens a gap where water and crumbs collect along the counter edge.

    Farmhouse Sink Installation in Lakeville, MN

    Apron-front sinks rest on a support frame built into the cabinet, not on the countertop. We build that frame from 2x4s or a steel bracket, set the sink dead level and leave the right reveal for the stone shop. We plan the drain early. Many farmhouse sinks drain at the back, and the trap has to work around that.

    Apron-Front Sinks in Lakeville, MN

    Apron sinks need a cabinet cut to fit the front panel, and the sink has to be in place before the counter is templated. We set the sink and frame early, so the fabricator measures from the actual bowl instead of a spec sheet. That avoids an uneven reveal. Cast iron versions can top 150 pounds, so we bring two people.

    Sink and Disposal Installed Together in Lakeville, MN

    A new sink is the cheapest time to add a disposal. The disposal mounts to the new flange, the trap is set to its outlet height and the dishwasher plug gets knocked out for the hose. You also need power. There needs to be a switched outlet under the sink.

    Stone shops vary on how they mount undermounts. Some epoxy threaded studs into the stone, others use a rail system, and we work with whichever one your countertop shop prefers. Either way, the bowl gets braced. The trap does not get connected until the adhesive cures.
