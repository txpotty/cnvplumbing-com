    Toilet Replacement and Upgrades in Lakeville, MN

    Swapping an old 3.5 gallon toilet for a modern one is the easiest water savings in the house. Pulling the old one takes around twenty minutes.     Commercial Flushometer Toilets in Lakeville

    Sloan and Zurn flushometer valves are the standard on commercial toilets for a reason. There is no tank to refill. We install manual and sensor-operated valves on floor-mount and wall-hung bowls, and we set the supply stop so the valve delivers its full rated volume without splashing or running on. Sensor valves cut down on touch points.

    Wall-Hung Toilet Installation in Lakeville, MN

    Wall-hung toilets need a wall at least 2 by 6 deep, or a furred-out chase built in front of an existing 2 by 4 wall. The floor underneath stays open. That makes mopping easier and lets you set the bowl height anywhere within the carrier's range, which is handy for tall households or anyone who wants a comfort-height seat without a tall bowl. Service happens through the flush plate.

    High-Efficiency Toilet Installation in Lakeville, MN

    High-efficiency toilets flush on 1.28 gallons or less, against 3.5 gallons or more for toilets from before the early 1990s. Check for the WaterSense label. A MaP rating of 800 grams or better is what we look for, since a weak low-flow toilet that needs double flushing saves no water at all. Dual-flush models add a lighter half flush.

    Smart Toilet Installation in Lakeville, MN

    Smart toilets with heated seats and built-in bidets need two things the old toilet did not: power and a dedicated water connection. Most need a GFCI outlet close enough for the cord. The water connection and mounting are ours, and when an outlet has to be added we schedule a licensed electrician instead of doing that wiring. Then we test every button.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Toilet Installation in Lakeville

    Labor for a standard toilet installation in Lakeville usually runs $250 to $600, not counting the toilet itself. Flange repairs add to that. Extra work such as a rotted flange or soft subfloor raises the price, and we quote it before we begin.

    Figure one to two hours for a standard floor-mount toilet. Some jobs run a full day. Wall-hung carriers and slab relocations open walls or floors, so they get booked as separate projects with cure time built in.

    Adding or moving a toilet almost always needs one. A simple swap usually goes without. An inspector checks the new drain and vent while things are still open, and we book that visit around your remodel schedule.

    TOTO and American Standard do well in independent flush tests, and Kohler is close behind, with parts at most supply houses. For a budget, Gerber and Mansfield hold up. The right pick depends on your rough-in and who uses the room.

    A one-piece toilet is cast as a single unit, so there is no tank-to-bowl joint to leak. It costs more and weighs more. A two-piece bolts the tank to the bowl with a rubber gasket, which makes it lighter to carry up stairs and cheaper to fix, since you can replace just the tank or just the bowl if one cracks. Both flush well.

    Two warranties apply. The toilet is covered by its maker. Parts usually get one to five years and the china often far longer, and our guarantee picks up any leak caused by the install.

    Just about any brand you can buy at a supply house or home center. We see a lot of TOTO and American Standard. Gerber and Mansfield are common in rentals because parts are cheap and easy to get, and we can usually tell you which one fits your budget and your rough-in after a short call. Bring us the model number.

    Rough-in is the distance from the finished wall to the center of the toilet drain. Twelve inches is the most common. Some older homes and tight bathrooms have 10 or 14 inch rough-ins, and a toilet built for the wrong distance either will not fit against the wall or leaves an awkward gap behind the tank. Measure from the wall itself, not the baseboard.
