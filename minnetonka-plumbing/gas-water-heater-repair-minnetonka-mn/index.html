    Tank Leaks and Anode Rods in Minnetonka

    Corrosion starts at the anode rod and works its way into the steel once the rod is spent. Water across much of Minnesota is hard, and many supplies also carry iron or manganese that discolor fixtures and laundry. The tech pulls the anode, checks what metal remains and swaps it if bare core wire shows. A fresh anode, a drained tank and a relief valve that seats properly can add years to a heater in a Minnetonka home that has not started leaking from the shell. Water from the shell seam is another story. At that point the tank has to go.

    Propane Water Heater Repair in Minnetonka

    Propane and natural gas heaters look the same but burn at different pressures. A propane tank needs its own orifice and a valve set for roughly 11 inches of water column. Natural gas runs closer to 7 inches, so a heater converted badly, or a regulator that drifted, gives you soot, a yellow flame and a pilot that keeps going out for no obvious reason. A manometer reading tells us the supply pressure. Wrong parts get swapped for the right ones.

    Burner Cleaning and Repair in Minnetonka, MN

    Yellow flame tips, soot on the burner door or a roaring sound at startup all mean the burner is not getting clean air. We pull the burner, brush the ports and blow out the orifice. We also confirm the orifice is drilled for the fuel you have, because a natural gas orifice on a propane heater, or the reverse, will soot up the flue and push carbon monoxide levels well past normal. You want a steady blue flame. Rust flakes on the burner tell us to look up the flue.

    Pilot Light Repair in Minnetonka

    The thermocouple is the first thing we test when a pilot will not stay lit. A millivolt meter tells us in about a minute whether it is producing enough current. If it reads fine, the next suspects are a clogged pilot orifice, a cracked piezo wire or a thermal cutoff that tripped because the combustion chamber overheated from blocked air. Most of these jobs run about an hour.
