    Well Pump Problems We Fix in Minnetonka, MN

    Submersible Well Pump Repair in Minnetonka

    Submersible pumps hang inside the well casing on drop pipe, often 100 feet or more below the wellhead. The usual signs are no water at all, weak pressure or a hum with no flow. We test the motor from the surface first. When a pull is needed, a hoist brings the pipe up safely so we can replace the motor, the impellers or the check valve.

    Pressure Tank Repair in Minnetonka, MN

    A waterlogged pressure tank makes the pump short cycle, turning on and off every few seconds. That rapid cycling burns out pumps early. We check the air charge, inspect the bladder and recharge or replace the tank, then confirm the pump runs at least a minute per cycle. Steadier pressure follows.

    Jet Pump Repair in Minnetonka, MN

    Shallow wells and booster systems often use jet pumps. Common problems are loss of prime, a leaking shaft seal and a worn impeller. Our crew in Minnetonka restores prime, replaces worn parts and checks the suction line for air leaks. That matters most where water levels rise and fall through the year, since a dropping level can starve the pump and cost it prime.

    Well Pump Maintenance & Repair Cost in Minnetonka, MN

    Maintenance costs far less than an emergency pull. A typical well pump repair in Minnetonka runs $400 to $1,500, while a yearly checkup costs a fraction of that. CNV Plumbing prices both up front. We check tank precharge, switch contacts, amp draw and the well cap, which catches most problems early.

    Well Pump Electrical Troubleshooting in Minnetonka, MN

    Electrical faults cause a big share of well calls, from a tripped breaker to a burned contact in the pressure switch. After that, we look at the capacitor and relay in the control box. We lock out the power before testing anything. All wiring and splices meet Minnesota electrical code before power goes back on.

    Well Pressure Tank Repair in Minnetonka

    Bladder tanks hold a cushion of air that lets the pump rest between cycles. A failed bladder lets the tank waterlog, and the pump begins to short cycle. We inspect the bladder, the switch and the tank connections. Proper sizing adds years to a pump.

    Submersible Pump Pulling and Repair in Minnetonka

    Sand wears impellers down over time, and a pump that once filled the tank in a minute starts taking three. The motor, the wire splice and the check valve are the other common failures. We test the motor and wire from the surface before deciding whether to pull. Replacement pumps get sized to the yield and depth of the well.

    Control Box and Electrical Repair in Minnetonka, MN

    Capacitors, relays and corroded terminals inside the control box cause many pump failures. Breaker trips usually come first. Licensed technicians test these parts safely with the power locked out, replace what failed and tighten every connection in the box. Wiring meets Minnesota code when the job is done.
