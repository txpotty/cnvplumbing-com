    Hydrojetting for Cast Iron Pipes in Plymouth, MN

    Older cast iron drains rust and scale from the inside, leaving a rough surface that grabs debris. A jet clears that crust. Thin or cracked cast iron gets gentler settings or a lining recommendation instead.

    Root Removal Jetting in Plymouth

    Shade trees and shrubs send roots toward small cracks in clay and cast iron pipe. A root nozzle cuts them out. Jetting does not stop roots from returning, because the crack is still there, but it clears the pipe completely and lets us see on camera whether a spot repair or a liner would stop the regrowth.

    Sewer Camera Inspection Before Hydrojetting in Plymouth

    Jetting a broken pipe can make the break worse. We camera every line before we set up the jetter. The video shows the pipe material, the condition of each joint, and any cracks, offsets or sags, which tells us the nozzle and pressure to use or whether we should not jet that line at all.

    Main Line Jetting in Plymouth, MN

    The lateral from your house to the street handles every drain in the house, so buildup there causes backups everywhere at once. The whole lateral gets jetted to the tap. Low spots where the pipe has settled hold water and collect sludge, so we spend extra passes there and mark the location on the camera footage in case a repair makes sense later.

    Hydrojetting vs. Snaking in Plymouth, MN

    Snaking pokes a hole through a clog. Jetting cleans the whole pipe. For a single clog in an otherwise clean line, snaking is cheaper and works fine, but for a line that clogs every few months, jetting removes the buildup that keeps catching debris.

    Tree Root Removal with Hydrojetting in Plymouth

    Roots find their way into sewer lines through cracked joints, and older clay pipe has a joint every few feet. We use a spinning root-cutter nozzle. The jet then flushes the debris to the main, and the camera marks the joints that let the roots in.

    Preventive Hydrojet Maintenance in Plymouth

    Preventive jetting pays off for lines that clog more than once a year. Camera findings set the interval. A line with heavy grease might need jetting every six months, while a sewer with a few root entry points might only need it every 18 to 24 months, and the footage tells us which.
