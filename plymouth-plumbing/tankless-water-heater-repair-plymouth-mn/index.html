    Frequently Asked Questions About Tankless Water Heater Repair in Plymouth

    Expect somewhere between $150 and $800 for most tankless repairs in Plymouth, based on the part and the time it takes to find the fault. Sensors are cheap. A board or exchanger costs real money. We give a written estimate after testing.

    A yearly flush and a clean inlet screen prevent most of the calls we get. Adding a softener helps if your water is hard.

    Descale yearly on hard water and clean the inlet screen two or three times a year. A softener or scale-reduction cartridge ahead of the heater slows scale a lot, and keeping the intake clear of leaves and lint heads off combustion faults. Check the vent cap after storms.

    In Plymouth, swapping a sensor or flame rod is normally permit-free. Changes to the gas line, venting or the unit itself typically need one, and we handle that filing.

    Each code points to one fault, like flame failure, overheating, scale or no ignition. The numbers differ from brand to brand, so a Rinnai 11 and a Navien E003 both mean the burner failed to light. We read them from the manufacturer's chart.

    Cleaning the inlet filter and wiping down the intake screen are fair homeowner jobs. Opening the sealed burner compartment is not, because a misaligned gasket or loose gas fitting there can leak exhaust or gas into the house, and many warranties are voided by unlicensed work. Leave that work to a licensed plumber.

    Most take one to three hours on site. A descale by itself runs about ninety minutes, while a part that has to be ordered, like a control board, usually means a second visit within a few business days. We tell you which before we leave.

    You can clean the inlet water filter and the air intake screen on most models by following the owner's manual. Gas valves, igniters and anything behind the sealed combustion cover should go to a licensed plumber, and DIY work there can void the manufacturer warranty. Leave the burner alone.
