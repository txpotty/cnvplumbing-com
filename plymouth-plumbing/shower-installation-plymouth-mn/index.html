    How We Install Showers in Plymouth, MN

    Budget Shower Upgrades in Plymouth, MN

    Budget showers can still be built right. A fiberglass or acrylic unit with a quality pressure-balance valve costs far less than a tiled shower, and when it is set on a mortar bed with a sealed drain, it lasts decades. Spend on the valve. You get the full price before we start.

    Tub-to-Shower Conversion in Plymouth, MN

    Tub-to-shower conversions are one of our most common jobs. We pull the tub, move the drain from the end of the tub to where the shower pan needs it and raise the valve to shower height, usually around 48 inches off the floor. Slab homes need concrete work. The new shower is safer to step into.

    Custom Shower Enclosure Plumbing in Plymouth

    Body sprays and steam units add lines and planning. Each spray needs its own supply, and the valve, the diverter and the pipe sizing all have to work together so the last spray in the line still gets decent pressure. Pressure varies by house. We test static pressure before designing the layout.

    Custom Shower System Installation in Plymouth, MN

    Multi-head showers depend on valve capacity. One head works fine off a standard valve, while multiple outlets need a thermostatic valve, volume controls and larger 3/4-inch supply lines. We plan the layout first. Then we check whether the water heater can keep up.

    Curbless Shower Installation in Plymouth

    Curbless showers need the floor lowered in the shower area. A wood floor gets dropped joists or a recessed pan, while a slab gets cut and lowered so the floor flows straight in. A linear drain often works best. We plan the slope with your contractor before demo.

    Shower Valve and Trim Installation in Plymouth, MN

    The valve sits behind the tile for decades. Each valve is set to the depth its trim requires, and we include integral stops whenever the valve supports them. Trim goes on after tile. Waiting keeps the trim clean during tiling.

    Rain and Multi-Head Shower Installation in Plymouth

    Rain heads pull up to 2.5 gallons per minute each. Pair a rain head with a handheld and body sprays and a 40-gallon water heater may run cold halfway through. We check the heater. Thermostatic valves with volume controls run the outlets.

    Gym and Locker Room Showers in Plymouth

    Gym showers run back to back from open to close. Commercial valves with metered or push-button controls keep run times in check, and the drains are sized for heavy use. Accessible stalls get handhelds on slide bars. Work is phased so part of the room stays open.
