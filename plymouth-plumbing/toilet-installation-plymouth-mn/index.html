    Floor-Mount and Wall-Hung Toilet Installs in Plymouth, MN

    Toilet Flange and Wax Ring Replacement in Plymouth, MN

    The closet flange is the ring that ties the toilet to the drain, and it is the weak point on most old installs. Cast iron flanges rust through at the bolt slots. PVC and ABS flanges crack when someone overtightens the bolts, and a repair ring screwed over the top is often all it takes to restore a solid anchor. Seal goes on last.

    Commercial Flushometer Toilets in Plymouth

    Sloan and Zurn flushometer valves are the standard on commercial toilets for a reason. They recover instantly between flushes. Manual and sensor valves go on both floor-mount and wall-hung bowls, with the control stop adjusted so the flush is full but does not splash. Sensor valves cut down on touch points.

    Pressure-Assisted Toilet Installation in Plymouth

    Pressure-assisted toilets have a sealed vessel inside the tank that uses line pressure to push the flush. Below about 20 psi, they flush poorly. Before suggesting one, we put a gauge on a hose bib, and we mention the noise, because the flush carries through the whole house. They rarely clog.

    Standard Toilet Installation in Plymouth

    A standard floor-mount toilet is a two-hour job when the flange is sound. We shut off the stop, drain the tank and pull the old bowl. Once the old wax is scraped off, the flange gets checked for cracks, new stainless bolts go in, and the new bowl is lowered straight onto a fresh seal. The old one leaves with us.

    Wall-Mounted Toilet Installation in Plymouth

    Wall-mounted toilets hang off a steel carrier bolted to the floor inside the wall. An access plate hides the tank. Because the carrier carries all the weight, we anchor its feet to the slab or framing first and check it for plumb before the drywall or tile goes up. Plan on a remodel, not a swap.

    Commercial Restroom Toilets in Plymouth, MN

    Restaurants and offices get heavy-duty fixtures and accessible stalls built to code. Downtime costs a business money. Work gets scheduled before opening or after close, with fixtures staged on site, so each stall is out of service only briefly. We work with your GC on inspections.

    New Construction Toilet Installation in Plymouth

    On new builds we set toilets after the finish floor is down, never before. A flange belongs flush with or just above finished flooring. Set it too early and the tile setter has to cut around it, which leaves the flange low and forces a thick seal or an extender later on. Each bowl gets a fresh seal.

    Bathroom Remodel Toilet Installation in Plymouth, MN

    New tile over old vinyl can raise a bathroom floor by half an inch or more. That leaves the old flange sitting low. We bring the flange up with a stacking extender or replace it outright, and if you are moving the toilet on a slab, that means cutting concrete to reroute the drain and patching it afterward. Moving it on a wood floor is easier.

    Toilet Installation Near You in Plymouth, MN

    Older Plymouth homes can hide surprises under the toilet, like a cast iron flange rusted thin or a subfloor gone soft from an old leak. Both get checked before anything is set. Soft plywood around the flange gets cut out and replaced with new subfloor, glued and screwed, so the toilet sits on something solid. It adds an hour, sometimes two.

    Toilet installers near you in Plymouth should be licensed and insured, and they should be willing to pull a permit when the job calls for one. Ask about both on the phone.     Rough-in is the distance from the finished wall to the center of the toilet drain. Twelve inches is the most common. You will also see 10 and 14 inch rough-ins, and buying the wrong one means the toilet will not fit or sits well off the wall. Measure from the wall itself, not the baseboard.

    For a replacement in the same spot, usually no. New bathrooms and additions usually do. Requirements differ by jurisdiction, so we call the building department first and pull the permit when the job needs it. That cost goes on the quote.

    Start with the rough-in, since that decides which models fit at all. Then pick round or elongated. An elongated bowl is roomier but adds about two inches of length, and that matters in a tight half bath. Check the MaP flush score too.

    Adding or moving a toilet almost always needs one. A simple swap usually goes without. An inspector checks the new drain and vent while things are still open, and we book that visit around your remodel schedule.

    Homeowners set basic toilets all the time. Flange height and bolt torque are where it goes wrong.
