    Well Pressure Tank Replacement in Edina

    A waterlogged pressure tank makes a healthy pump look sick. If the pump clicks on every time someone runs a faucet for ten seconds, the bladder in the tank has probably failed and the pump is short cycling itself to death. We check the air charge with a gauge. Precharge should sit about 2 psi below the cut-in pressure.

    Constant Pressure Pump Systems in Edina

    Franklin SubDrive and Grundfos SQE are the two constant pressure systems we install most. They get by with a much smaller tank because the drive matches pump speed to demand. Soft starts also cut wear on the motor. The upfront cost is higher.

    Control Box and Pressure Switch Replacement in Edina, MN

    Not every dry faucet means a dead pump. A pressure switch with pitted contacts or a plugged sensing tube can stop the pump even though nothing is wrong down the well. A new switch runs about $30 in parts. You get to see the old part.

    Pulling a Submersible Pump in Edina

    Pulling a submersible means lifting the pump and every length of drop pipe out through the pitless adapter, sometimes hundreds of feet of it. We check the torque arrestor and every splice on the way up. Most jobs are done in a day.

    Solar Well Pump Systems in Edina, MN

    Solar well pumps make sense for livestock tanks and cabins where running power from the meter would cost more than the pump itself. A few panels feed a DC pump through a controller while the sun is up. You store water, not electricity. Panel count comes from how many gallons you need a day.

    No-Water Calls in Edina

    Frequently Asked Questions About Well Pump Replacement in Edina

    Plan on $1,500 to $3,500 for a typical residential job. A deep well or a bigger pump pushes the price up, and so does a new tank or wire. We quote after testing.

    Pump manufacturers usually warranty their units for 1 to 5 years against defects. A few manufacturers add time when a licensed installer sets the pump. We add a labor warranty of our own.

    Our usual picks are Franklin and Grundfos for submersibles, Goulds for jet pumps, and Zoeller for some shallow well work. Matching your current brand is usually easy. Budget matters too.

    One to five years from the manufacturer, typically. We also provide a labor warranty.

    Keep the tank precharge right and fix leaks fast. A tank low on air or a toilet that runs will keep the pump starting all night, and every start wears the motor. A sediment filter helps if your well makes sand.

    Shallow well pumps, usually jet pumps, sit above ground and pull water by suction from about 25 feet or less. Submersibles go down in the water and push it to the surface. Physics sets that 25-foot limit.

    Location is the difference. A jet pump sits above ground and pulls water up by suction, which limits it to shallow wells, while a submersible hangs in the water and pushes it up from hundreds of feet. Submersibles are more efficient at depth.
