    Residential Sewer Cleaning in Apple Valley

    Household sewers clog slowly. Grease, hair and wipes build up over months. Each flush adds a little more to the pile at a rough joint or a low spot, until the 4 inch line is down to a trickle and the lowest drain in the house starts to back up, and we clear it with a sectional cable or a small jetter sized for the pipe.

    Jetting a Sewer Line in Apple Valley, MN

    Grease and scale are jetting work. A cable only scratches them. The rear jets drive the hose forward while the spray scours the wall, so the line ends up much closer to its original size.

    Preventative Sewer Maintenance in Apple Valley

    Roots come back after cutting. Plan on regrowth in 12 to 18 months. Putting a root-prone main on a yearly cleaning schedule keeps the growth small enough that it never stops the line, and it costs far less than an after-hours emergency call.

    Hydro Jetting for Sewer Lines in Apple Valley

    Jetting cleans the whole pipe wall. Cabling just opens a hole. A jetter sends high-pressure water around the full circumference of the pipe, removing buildup that a cable would leave behind.

    Clay and Orangeburg Sewer Lines in Apple Valley

    Older properties may still have clay or Orangeburg pipe. Each fails in its own way. Clay joints invite roots, and Orangeburg, a tar-paper pipe from the mid-1900s, flattens under the weight of the soil over time, so we clean these lines carefully and map the joints and soft spots on camera.

    Tree Root Removal in Apple Valley, MN Sewers

    Roots get in through tiny gaps. Then they fill the pipe. A root saw on a sectional cable cuts the mass loose so we can pull it out through the cleanout, and a jetter can follow to flush out the fine root hairs left on the pipe wall, and a camera shows where they are getting in.
