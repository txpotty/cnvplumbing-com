    Hidden Wall and Ceiling Leaks in Apple Valley

    Bubbling paint, soft drywall and a musty smell come up on most of our calls. All of them mean water. Thermal cameras find the wet area, and a moisture meter confirms it. Acoustic tools help when the leak is on a pressurized line, and once the source is pinned down we cut a single neat access opening instead of chasing the stain across the wall.

    Pool Leak Detection in Apple Valley

    A pool losing more than a quarter inch a day beyond evaporation probably has a leak. We start with a bucket test. Then we pressure test the suction and return lines, use an underwater hydrophone to listen, and dye test around the skimmer, lights and main drain so we can point to the exact fitting or crack.

    Main Water Line Leak Detection in Apple Valley

    A soft wet patch near the sidewalk or a sinking spot over the line can mean the service pipe is leaking. Old galvanized and copper lines fail most. We map the pipe with a line tracer and listen for the leak with a ground microphone, so excavation stays to one small hole instead of a trench across the yard. Then we patch or replace.

    Slab Leak Signs in Apple Valley, MN

    A meter that never rests, cracked tile and warm floor spots are the common signs. So is the sound of water with everything off. We isolate hot and cold lines with pressure tests, then trace and listen through the slab, then lay out the spot repair, reroute and repipe choices with a price on each.

    Sewer Line Leak Detection in Apple Valley, MN

    A cracked drain line leaks wastewater into the soil, which can show up as a sewer smell in the yard, a soggy patch or slow drains. We use a camera for these. The camera goes in at the cleanout and runs to the main, recording cracks, offset joints and root entry in PVC, clay and cast iron, with a locator giving the exact depth and distance.

    PEX and Copper Leak Detection in Apple Valley, MN

    On a ground microphone, copper leaks are easy to hear. PEX is quieter, since plastic pipe does not carry sound well, so we lean more on thermal scans, moisture readings and tracer gas when the house is plumbed in it. We handle both every week. Pinholes in copper and failed crimp fittings in PEX top the list of what we find.

    Leaks in Walls and Ceilings in Apple Valley, MN

Thermal cameras show the cool wet area, a moisture meter confirms it and a borescope through a small hole lets us see the actual pipe before any big opening is made.
