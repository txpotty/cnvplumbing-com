    Frequently Asked Questions About Drain Repairs in Coon Rapids

    Clearing a clog usually runs $150 to $400. Pipe repairs cost more. Main line repairs can be anywhere from $800 to $5,000 or more, especially when the pipe is buried and has to be excavated to reach the damaged section. We price it once we know the cause.

    A dry trap is the most common cause. Unused fixtures dry out. A cracked drain fitting, a failed wax ring under the toilet or a blocked vent can also let sewer gas into the room, and we track it down with a smoke test or a close visual check. Then we seal it.

    We install PVC and ABS for most new drain work. Cast iron where needed. Cast iron still makes sense in some multi-story walls where noise matters, and we tie new pipe into existing metal drains with listed transition fittings. Every part meets code.

    PVC, ABS, cast iron, galvanized and copper drain all show up in Minnesota homes. We work on all of them. Joining new plastic to old metal takes a shielded transition coupling sized to the outside diameter of each pipe, and getting that fitting right is what keeps the repair from leaking later. We stock the usual couplings.

    Age and corrosion lead the list. Settling ground plays a part. Tree roots, temperature swings, heavy loads over buried pipe, plus bad installation such as skipped primer or unsupported runs, can all break a drain early. Most failures have more than one cause.

    Call us if a plunger or a small hand snake has not cleared it. Repeat clogs too. Multiple slow drains, sewer smell or water coming up in a different fixture than the one you are using point to a blockage or defect past the reach of a home snake. Those call for professional equipment.

    A trap swap or a fixture drain fix is usually an hour or two. Main lines take longer. Replacing a section of main line can take a full day or several days, especially if permits, inspections or excavation under a slab are involved. The schedule is in the quote.

    Slow drains, gurgling and sewer smell are the big three warnings. So is water under the sink.
