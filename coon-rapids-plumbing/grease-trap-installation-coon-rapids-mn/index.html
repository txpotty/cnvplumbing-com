    Hydro-Mechanical Grease Trap Installation in Coon Rapids, MN

    Frequently Asked Questions About Grease Trap Installation in Coon Rapids

    Small under-sink hydromechanical traps usually land between $1,000 and $3,000 installed, and buried interceptors with excavation commonly run $5,000 to $20,000 or more. The spread comes from access and how much concrete or paving we cut. Permits add a little. We quote in writing after a site visit, with the trap, the labor and the permit fees listed as separate lines.

    In everyday talk the two names get swapped. Strictly, a grease trap is the small indoor unit rated in gallons per minute, and an interceptor is the large tank rated in gallons of capacity. Codes vary on the wording. Many codes say hydromechanical interceptor and gravity interceptor instead, and we match the local wording on the permit.

    From first call to final inspection, most indoor jobs take one to two weeks, and most of that is waiting on the permit and the part. Our time on site is usually one day. Outdoor jobs run longer. Gravity interceptors can take three to six weeks end to end because of plan review and tank delivery.

    As close to the grease-producing fixtures as the space allows, so grease does not cool and coat the pipe before it reaches the trap. Leave room to open the lid and clean it without moving equipment. Never under a cook line. Outdoor tanks should sit where the hauler can reach them and nothing gets parked on the lids.

    The common rule is to clean before grease and solids fill 25 percent of the trap's depth. For a small indoor trap in a busy kitchen, that can be weekly. Outdoor tanks go longer. A gravity interceptor typically gets pumped monthly to quarterly, and some sewer programs set the schedule for you.

    You get the manufacturer's warranty on the unit and a written labor warranty from us on the installation. Factory terms vary, so we hand you the registration card or file it for you. Keep your cleaning logs. Makers may ask for service records before paying a claim.

    An under-sink hydromechanical trap with good access is usually a half-day job, including the flow control and a leak test. Recessing one into the slab adds a day or two for saw-cutting and patching. Outdoor tanks are different. Plan on two to five days for a gravity interceptor once the permit is in hand, depending on depth, soil and how much paving we have to restore.

    Plan on a permit. Both the building department and the sewer authority usually have a say. We file both when needed and give you copies of the approvals, which your health inspector may ask to see at opening. Unpermitted traps cause trouble at lease transfer.
