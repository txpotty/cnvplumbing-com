    Video Camera Sewer Inspection in St. Cloud

    A self-leveling camera goes in at the cleanout or through a pulled toilet. The full length gets recorded. Older homes in St. Cloud often hide two or three problems in one line, and the video catches all of them.

    Sewer Vent Pipe Repair in St. Cloud, MN

    A plugged vent makes drains gurgle and traps run dry. Then the sewer smell shows up. Bird nests, leaves and ice can block a roof vent, and older galvanized or cast iron vent stacks crack inside the walls where nobody sees them, which is why we look at the vent whenever the lateral checks out fine. Fixes range from a quick clearing to a new section of stack.

    Pipe Burst & Collapse Repair in St. Cloud, MN

    A collapsed sewer pipe stops all flow at the point of failure. Every drain backs up. Collapses usually come from corroded cast iron, crushed clay under a driveway or tar-paper pipe that has finally flattened, and the camera tells us exactly where the pipe gave way and how deep it sits. We dig at the mark and replace the broken length with PVC. Lining cannot fix a full collapse.

    Sewer Line Backups and Blockages in St. Cloud

    Water coming up in the tub when the washer drains is a classic main line symptom. The clog is past the branches.     Sewer Repair Cost Comparison in St. Cloud, MN

    A spot repair is the cheapest fix when the rest of the line is sound. Per foot, lining runs higher.     Traditional Sewer Line Replacement in St. Cloud

    Some lines are too far gone for a liner. Crushed pipe cannot be lined. When the camera shows a collapse, a large offset or a belly deep enough to hold several inches of water, we open a trench, remove the old pipe and lay new Schedule 40 PVC on proper bedding at a quarter inch of fall per foot. Older homes often need this on at least one section. The dig is staged so the house is without a sewer for as little time as possible.

    Sewer Pipe Relining in St. Cloud, MN

    Relining builds a new pipe inside the old one. The old pipe gets jetted clean before anything else. A flexible liner saturated with epoxy is pulled or inverted into place, inflated against the old wall and left to cure, so every crack and leaking joint is covered by one jointless pipe. The finished pipe loses a little diameter. On a four-inch residential line, that rarely matters.
