    Tankless Water Heater Descaling in St. Paul

    Tankless units need descaling. Scale builds up inside the heat exchanger, especially with hard water, and the unit starts throwing error codes, running lukewarm or cutting out mid-shower long before it actually wears out. We pump descaler through the service valves. The inlet filter, venting and gas line get checked too.

    Whole-Home Plumbing Inspection in St. Paul, MN

    Room by room, fixture by fixture. We check under every sink for drips, test toilet fill valves and flappers, run every faucet and shower, look at the washer hoses and read the house water pressure at a hose bib. Small fixes happen on the spot. Larger repairs get a written price.

    Water Heater Flush and Inspection in St. Paul

    Popping or rumbling means sediment. Minerals settle on the tank bottom, trap heat and cause the popping you hear when it fires. We flush it out. We descale tankless units through their service valves. Regular service helps keep the warranty valid.

    Scheduled Service for Businesses in St. Paul

    Commercial plumbing takes a beating. Large heaters, restrooms, backflow devices and kitchen lines get checked on a set schedule. Inspections go smoother. You get records for every visit.

    Tank and Tankless Heater Tune-Ups in St. Paul

    Mineral-heavy water shortens water heater life. We drain sediment, check the anode rod, inspect the burner or elements and confirm the relief valve opens and reseats, whether the unit is a standard tank or a tankless model on the wall. Most failures give warning. We spot the signs early.

    Sump Pump Maintenance in St. Paul, MN

    Summer thunderstorms can drop heavy rain in a short time, causing sewer backups in older neighborhoods. A sump pump sits idle for months and then has to work hard all at once, so we test the float switch, clean debris out of the pit and confirm the discharge line runs clear to daylight. The check valve gets a look. Battery backups get tested too.

    Hot Water Tank Service in St. Paul, MN

    Gas and electric tanks need different checks. On gas units we look at the burner flame, the flue draft and the gas connection for leaks, and on electric units we test the elements and the thermostats and check the wiring for heat damage. Sediment comes out either way. Mineral buildup on the bottom wastes fuel and can lead to early tank failure.
