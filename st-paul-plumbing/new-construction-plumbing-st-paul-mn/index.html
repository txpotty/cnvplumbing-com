Top-out happens after framing and takes three to five days, then trim waits until cabinets and counters are in, which can be months later on a custom home. Trim is one or two days.

    Extremely cold, long winters are the biggest stress on plumbing in Minnesota, and incoming water stays cold for much of the year. Frozen pipes are a constant winter concern in Minnesota, and deep frost can even reach buried water service lines during severe winters. Pipe routes and insulation get chosen to match. That can mean keeping supply lines out of exterior walls, insulating attic runs, and setting frost-free hose bibs that drain back inside the heated space. Attics get the most attention.

    Most jurisdictions inspect plumbing three times. The underground inspection happens before the slab is poured, with drains under a water or air test. Top-out gets inspected before insulation, and the final comes after fixtures are set and running. Some add a gas test.

    Scheduling causes most of the trouble. Pushed pours and framing changes tend to pile phases together. Every change gets priced in writing before we move pipe.
