    Underground Water Line Work in St. Paul, MN

    Water Line Repair at the Meter in St. Paul, MN

    The meter box is where a lot of leaks start. Fittings in the box loosen with ground movement and traffic. Before we touch anything, we figure out whether the leak is before or after the meter, because the water provider owns the meter and everything on the street side. When it is on your side, we repair it. That often means new fittings and a new customer shutoff valve on the same visit.

    Main Water Line Repair in St. Paul

    Your main line is the pipe from the meter to the house, usually 3/4 inch or 1 inch, buried anywhere from a foot to several feet down depending on frost depth. A leak here shows up as a soggy strip of lawn and a meter dial that never rests. We start by closing the house shutoff and watching the dial. A moving dial with the house valve closed means an underground leak. Then a ground microphone narrows the spot, and we dig one hole instead of a trench across your yard.

    Slab Leak Water Line Repair in St. Paul

    A slab leak is a supply pipe leaking under the concrete floor of the house. Older homes in Minnesota often still have galvanized supply pipe and cast iron drains, both of which corrode and clog with age. Signs include a warm spot on the floor, the sound of running water and a meter that moves with everything off. Acoustic gear and a thermal camera tell us where it is. From there you have three choices: break the slab at the spot, reroute the line through the walls or attic, or skip the old under-slab pipe and repipe overhead with PEX. We price each one for you.

    Emergency Water Line Repair in St. Paul, MN

    First thing on a burst line is shutting the water off, at the house valve if it works and at the curb stop if it does not. Water pouring into a yard undermines everything around it. CNV Plumbing sends a plumber with a curb key, a pump and repair couplings. We can usually stop it within the hour. If the repair will run long, a temporary bypass keeps water flowing to the house during the repair.

    Emergency Water Line Break Repair in St. Paul, MN

    A full break can flood the yard and leave the house dry at the same time. Shutting the water off comes first. The crew pumps the hole, finds the break and decides whether a coupling will do or whether a section needs replacing, setting up a temporary supply to the house if the job will take a while. Most breaks are fixed the same day. We photograph the damage for your insurance or HOA.

    Galvanized Pipe Water Line Repair in St. Paul, MN

    Galvanized steel service lines rust from the inside out. By the time one leaks, the rest of the pipe is close behind. We can clamp or replace a short section to get you through, which can hold for a while if the pipe is not too far gone. More often we recommend replacing the whole run from the meter to the house with copper or PEX. You get your flow back as a bonus.

    Pressure Regulator and Shutoff Repair in St. Paul

    High static pressure shortens the life of the service line and every fixture behind it. Most codes cap it at 80 psi. We gauge the pressure at a hose bib, then swap a failed regulator for a new Watts or Zurn Wilkins valve when it reads too high. Old gate valves that will not close get replaced with full-port ball valves. That way you can actually shut the water off.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Line Repair in St. Paul

    A minor leak repair often starts around $500. Replacing the whole line is a bigger number. A meter-to-house replacement or a trenchless pull can top $5,000 when the run is long, deep or under hardscape, and the price moves with pipe type, depth and whether we trench or bore. We quote it in writing after the inspection.

    Most replacements get Type K copper or PEX. For pulled lines we use HDPE. Copper lasts a very long time and resists punctures, and PEX flexes with the soil, tolerates freezing better and goes in faster. We recommend one based on your soil, line length, budget and what your local code allows in St. Paul.

    A typical spot repair runs two to four hours, from locating the leak to filling the hole. Replacements take longer. Deep lines, long runs and jobs under concrete or pavers can push the job into a second or third day. You get a realistic timeline when we hand you the quote.

    Watch the bill first. A sudden jump with no change in use is the classic clue. Other signs include low pressure everywhere, soggy ground in dry weather, discolored water and a hissing sound near the meter. On a slab home, a warm spot on the floor can mean the leak is under the house.

    You can tighten an exposed fitting at a hose bib. The buried line is a different job. They need the leak located, utility marking, careful excavation and burial-rated fittings. A mistake means a bigger leak and the same hole dug twice.
