    Gas Valve and Thermostat Repair in St. Paul, MN

    Lukewarm showers and a tank that never catches up often trace back to the gas control. The tech checks the dial, the immersion sensor and the outlet pressure with a manometer. On most residential tanks the thermostat lives inside the gas valve body, so a bad sensor or a stuck magnet usually means replacing the whole control rather than one small part. That swap takes about an hour. The new valve gets a leak test at every joint.

    Pilot Light and Burner Issues in St. Paul

    A pilot that lights, holds for ten seconds and then goes out is almost always a thermocouple or a weak magnet in the gas valve. A pilot that will not light at all is more often a clogged orifice or a dead igniter. Main burner problems show up another way. The pilot stays lit but the main flame never comes on, which points to the thermostat side of the valve, a tripped thermal cutoff or a flame arrestor so clogged with lint that the burner starves for air. Each part gets tested in that sequence.

    Propane Water Heater Repair in St. Paul

    Propane and natural gas heaters look the same but burn at different pressures. A propane tank needs its own orifice and a valve set for roughly 11 inches of water column. Natural gas runs closer to 7 inches, so a heater converted badly, or a regulator that drifted, gives you soot, a yellow flame and a pilot that keeps going out for no obvious reason. We check pressure with a manometer. Wrong parts get swapped for the right ones.

    Tank Leaks and Anode Rods in St. Paul

    Corrosion starts at the anode rod and works its way into the steel once the rod is spent. Water across much of Minnesota is hard, and many supplies also carry iron or manganese that discolor fixtures and laundry. The tech pulls the anode, checks what metal remains and swaps it if bare core wire shows. A fresh anode, a drained tank and a relief valve that seats properly can add years to a heater in a St. Paul home that has not started leaking from the shell. A wet bottom seam is different. That tank needs replacing.

    Gas Water Heater Noise Diagnosis in St. Paul

    Rumbling or popping from the bottom of the tank is water boiling under a layer of sediment. Hard water and years without a flush both make it worse. We drain the tank through the bottom valve, stir up the settled scale with short bursts of cold water and keep going until it runs clear, which on a neglected tank can take a while. Whistling points somewhere else. A relief valve or a half-closed supply valve is the usual cause.
