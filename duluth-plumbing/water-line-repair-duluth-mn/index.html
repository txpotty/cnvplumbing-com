    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Line Repair in Duluth

    Spot repairs on an accessible line usually run $350 to $1,500. The deeper the pipe, the higher the price.     Small spot repairs often do not need one. A new service line almost always does. Rules vary by jurisdiction in Duluth, so CNV Plumbing looks up the rule for your address and handles the permit if needed. The inspector sees the trench before it is backfilled.

    Keep trees and shrubs with big roots well away from the line. Mark the pipe route somewhere. A pressure gauge on a hose bib tells you if the regulator is failing, and anything over 80 psi is worth fixing before it starts wearing on the line. On galvanized lines, budgeting for a new line is cheaper than an emergency.

    We dig a pit at each end of the old line. First, a cable is threaded through. A bursting head on the cable splits the old line outward, and new HDPE pipe follows right behind it into the same space. It only works when the old pipe is a fairly straight run, and most home service lines qualify.

    You can tighten an exposed fitting at a hose bib. The buried line is a different job. They need the leak located, 811 marking, safe digging around the gas and power lines nearby and code-approved fittings made up correctly. A mistake means a bigger leak and the same hole dug twice.

    Watch the bill first. A sudden jump with no change in use is the classic clue. Other signs include low pressure everywhere, soggy ground in dry weather, discolored water and a hissing sound near the meter. On a slab home, a warm spot on the floor can mean the leak is under the house.

    Age is the biggest cause. Old pipe corrodes. Shifting soil, tree roots, high water pressure and freeze-thaw cycles all add stress, and an old line gets damaged by excavation work more often than most people realize. Poor original installation is another, such as pipe laid on sharp rock.
