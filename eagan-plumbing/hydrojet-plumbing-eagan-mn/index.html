    The main sewer carries every drain in the house, so a blockage there backs up the lowest fixture first. We jet the full main to the street tap. On a 4-inch line we use a heavier nozzle with more flow, which moves the loosened debris downstream instead of leaving it to settle in a low spot.

    Tree Root Removal with Hydrojetting in Eagan

    Roots find their way into sewer lines through cracked joints, and older clay pipe has a joint every few feet. We use a spinning root-cutter nozzle.     Sewer Lateral Jetting in Eagan, MN

    The lateral from your house to the street handles every drain in the house, so buildup there causes backups everywhere at once. The whole lateral gets jetted to the tap. Settled low spots get extra passes, and we mark them on the video for future reference.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Hydrojet Plumbing in Eagan

    Residential hydrojetting in Eagan typically costs $400 to $900, including a camera inspection before and after. A snake is cheaper per visit. Because jetting removes the buildup instead of punching through it, many homeowners end up spending less over a few years than they would on repeated snaking for the same line.

    Grease, mineral scale, sludge, soap buildup and roots all come out with jetting. Wipes and paper too. It also clears sand and silt from yard and storm drains, and it works on solid objects only if they are small enough to break up and flush, so a toy or a lost tool usually has to be retrieved instead.

    Every few years is plenty for most homes. Restaurants need it far more. Busy commercial kitchens often jet every three to six months, and apartment buildings with shared stacks may jet once or twice a year depending on what the camera shows.

    We run professional jetters from manufacturers like Spartan Tool and RIDGID. Smaller cart jetters cover most homes. Trailer jetters with larger water tanks and higher flow handle sewer mains and commercial lines, and we pair them with RIDGID SeeSnake cameras for the before and after inspection.

    We do not recommend it. Jetters can injure people. Professional jetters run at pressures that can cut skin, and without a camera you cannot tell whether the pipe is sound enough to jet, so a DIY attempt can injure you or turn a clog into a broken pipe.

    No approval is needed for routine jetting. It is treated as maintenance. Permits only apply if we move on to a repair or a new cleanout.

    Plan on two to three hours for a typical home. Camera work is included. Commercial jobs can take half a day, so we schedule them outside business hours.

    It is safe for most sound PVC, ABS, cast iron and clay. Settings change by material. Fragile or broken pipe gets low pressure or none, so the camera always comes first.
