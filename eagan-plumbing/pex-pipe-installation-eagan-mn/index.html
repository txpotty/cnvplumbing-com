    What Goes Into a PEX Install in Eagan, MN

    PEX-A, PEX-B, and PEX-C Systems in Eagan, MN

    There are three grades of PEX, named for how the plastic is cross-linked at the factory. PEX-A is the most flexible and joins with expansion rings, PEX-B is stiffer and joins with crimp rings or cinch clamps, and PEX-C sits closer to B in feel. Every one is listed for potable water. We match the pipe to the fittings and tools from one maker so the warranty stays intact.

    PEX for Water Line Replacement in Eagan

    Your service line runs from the meter or well to the house, often a foot and a half to three feet underground. Because PEX comes on a long coil, the whole run from the meter to the foundation can go in with zero buried fittings. Freeze and thaw movement can shift older sewer laterals, and tree roots often find their way into the joints. It gets bedded in sand, not rocky backfill. Sharp rock against plastic pipe is how a new line fails early.

    PEX-A Expansion Fitting Systems in Eagan, MN

    With PEX-A, the tech slips a ring over the pipe end and stretches both with a battery tool. The fitting is pushed in while the pipe is open, and the plastic's memory shrinks it back around the barb, sealing tighter during the first minute. Cold weather slows it. Joints get time to set before we pressurize.

    PEX-B Crimp and Clamp Systems in Eagan

    PEX-B is stiffer, costs less per foot, and joins with copper crimp rings or stainless cinch clamps over brass or poly-alloy fittings. A crimp joint is only as good as the tool, so we check every ring with a go/no-go gauge, and a ring that does not pass gets cut out and redone. It is a five-second check. Skipping it causes most crimp leaks.

    PEX-A vs. PEX-B Installation in Eagan

    Homeowners ask us which one is better, and the honest answer depends on the job. PEX-A makes tighter bends, can be heat-fixed if it kinks, and its fittings flow better, while PEX-B is cheaper and its crimps hold just as well when each one is gauged. Either one lasts for decades. For tight attics we lean toward PEX-A.

    Uponor PEX System Installation in Eagan

    Uponor's ProPEX system is PEX-A joined with an expansion tool and a cold-expansion ring. The tech expands the pipe and ring together, pushes the fitting in, and the plastic shrinks back around the barb over the next few seconds, tighter than when it started. There is no crimp gauge to check. The fittings have a wide bore, so flow loss through each joint stays low.

Each unit can get its own manifold and shutoff, so a leak in one apartment shuts down one apartment instead of the whole stack. Fire-rated walls need care.
