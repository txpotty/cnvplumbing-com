    Frequently Asked Questions About Backflow Preventer Installation in Maple Grove

    Residential PVBs usually cost $250 to $700 installed. For bigger homes and businesses, double checks and RPZs run $800 to $3,500 and up, depending on size and whether you need an enclosure.

    An RPZ protects high-hazard connections where the contaminant could make someone sick. A DCVA covers low-hazard connections with non-toxic contaminants, and the RPZ adds a relief valve that dumps water between its checks, which the double check does not have. The hazard decides it.

    Most water providers require an annual test by a certified backflow tester. The test confirms each check and relief valve still works, and we can put you on a reminder list so next year's test happens before the provider sends a notice. Missing the test can mean a shutoff.

    A permit is often required for new assemblies, especially on commercial properties. Local rules get checked, and we pull the permit when required.

    Water running from the relief valve on an RPZ is the clearest sign. Other signs include leaks at the body and low pressure past the device, and discolored or foul-smelling water means you should call for an inspection in Maple Grove the same day. Do not wait on bad water.

    Residential installs typically take two to four hours, test included. Large commercial assemblies can take a full day, especially if the piping has to be reconfigured.

    Testable assemblies have to be installed to code and certified by a licensed tester. Mount it at the wrong height or angle and it fails, and a device that does not protect your water can expose your family and the public supply to whatever is on the other side. Hire a licensed plumber.

    Our usual brands are Watts, Febco, Zurn and Apollo. Each meets the national standards providers ask for, and testers in most areas know them well, which makes the annual test faster and repairs cheaper. Familiar parts save money.
