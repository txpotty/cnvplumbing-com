    Sewer Cleaning Versus Sewer Repair in Maple Grove, MN

    Cleaning cannot fix a broken pipe. A crack stays a crack. If your sewer backs up every few months, the camera usually shows a belly, a crack or a collapsed section, and we compare the yearly cost of cleanings to a spot repair, a liner or a replacement, with the numbers in writing.

    Video Inspection Before Cleaning in Maple Grove, MN

    Some lines need a look first. Old pipe especially. A camera run before cleaning shows whether the line has roots, a broken section or a grease plug, which tells us whether to cable, jet or stop and talk about repair.

    Cast Iron Sewer Cleaning in Maple Grove, MN

    Cast iron rots from the inside. Older homes in Minnesota often still have galvanized supply pipe and cast iron drains, both of which corrode and clog with age. The bottom of the pipe wears first. Aggressive cutters can break through thin spots, so we use descaling chains and careful jetting made for older iron, which restores flow and gives a clear view of the remaining pipe wall on camera.

    Emergency Sewer Cleaning in Maple Grove

    Sewage on the floor cannot wait. We take emergency calls at night. A tech arrives with a main line machine, protective gear and a wet vac, ready to clear roots or a collapsed section and stop the backup before it spreads into more rooms.

    Jetting a Sewer Line in Maple Grove, MN

    Grease and scale are jetting work. Cables leave them behind. A jetter nozzle with rear-facing jets pulls itself down the line and blasts the buildup off the pipe wall, restoring close to the full diameter of the pipe and slowing the next clog.

    Commercial Sewer Cleaning in Maple Grove, MN

    Commercial lines take a beating. Restaurants fight grease every day. In offices and shops, paper and odd objects flushed in restrooms are the usual cause of a stopped lateral, and our heavy jetters handle 6 inch commercial pipe without much trouble.

    Dealing with Grease Buildup in Maple Grove, MN

    Grease hardens as it cools. It shrinks the opening a little every week. A greasy main slowly loses diameter until it cannot carry a full washer load or a bathtub draining at once, and jetting is the right fix.
