    Main Water Line Repair in Maple Grove

    Your main line is the pipe from the meter to the house, usually 3/4 inch or 1 inch, buried anywhere from a foot to several feet down depending on frost depth. When it fails you tend to see wet ground, a spinning meter and weak pressure at every tap. We start by closing the house shutoff and watching the dial. If it still moves, the leak is buried. Then a ground microphone narrows the spot, so the crew opens one small pit rather than trenching the whole run.

    Pipe Bursting and Pulling in Maple Grove

    Pipe bursting means splitting the old line in place while the new one follows behind it. Straight runs are ideal. A cone-shaped head breaks the old pipe outward, and a length of HDPE trails behind it in the same path. You end up with a fresh line and two small pits, and the lawn, sidewalk and driveway in between stay where they are, which is the whole reason people pick this method over a trench.

    PEX and Copper Water Line Repair in Maple Grove

    Copper and PEX fail in different places. Copper tends to pinhole where a rock rubs it or where the water chemistry eats the wall from inside. PEX usually fails at a fitting. We cut out the bad section of copper and sweat or press in a new length, and for PEX we use the matching crimp or expansion tool so the joint is made the way the manufacturer intends.

    PEX Water Line Repair in Maple Grove, MN

    PEX itself rarely fails underground, but the joints and the first few feet near the house take a beating. Rats and gophers sometimes chew it. A shovel or a fence post auger can nick it, and pipe left in the sun before it was buried can get brittle. We cut out the damaged section and install new pipe with crimp, clamp or expansion fittings, matched to the PEX brand already in the ground.

    Galvanized Pipe Water Line Repair in Maple Grove, MN

    Galvanized steel service lines rust from the inside out. By the time one leaks, the rest of the pipe is close behind. We can clamp or replace a short section to get you through, and sometimes that buys a few good years. More often we recommend replacing the whole run from the meter to the house with copper or PEX. You get your flow back as a bonus.

    Slab Leak Water Line Repair in Maple Grove

    A slab leak is a supply pipe leaking under the concrete floor of the house. Mid-century houses usually have copper supply lines, while newer construction typically uses PEX and plastic drains. Signs include a warm spot on the floor, the sound of running water and a meter that moves with everything off. Acoustic gear and a thermal camera tell us where it is. From there you have three choices: break the slab at the spot, reroute the line through the walls or attic, or skip the old under-slab pipe and repipe overhead with PEX. We price each one for you.

    Water Line Repair at the Meter in Maple Grove, MN

    The meter box is where a lot of leaks start. Fittings in the box loosen with ground movement and traffic. Before we touch anything, we figure out whether the leak is before or after the meter, since the utility is in charge of the meter and the pipe feeding it. If it is ours to fix, we fix it. That often means new fittings and a new customer shutoff valve on the same visit.

    Emergency Water Line Break Repair in Maple Grove, MN

    A full break can flood the yard and leave the house dry at the same time. We shut it down first. The crew pumps the hole, finds the break and decides whether a coupling will do or whether a section needs replacing, and if the repair will run past a few hours we set a temporary bypass to the house. Same-day repairs are the norm. We photograph the damage for your insurance or HOA.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Line Repair in Maple Grove

    Price depends mostly on depth, access and pipe material. A shallow spot repair is cheap. Most single-leak repairs land between $300 and $800, while a full main line replacement can run $1,500 to $6,000 or more depending on length and what sits on top of the pipe. You get a written estimate after we locate the leak.

    Small spot repairs often do not need one. A new service line almost always does. Rules vary by jurisdiction in Maple Grove, so CNV Plumbing looks up the rule for your address and handles the permit if needed. An inspection happens with the pipe still exposed.

    In most places the water provider owns the meter and the pipe on the street side of it. You own the rest. Some providers draw the line at the curb stop instead, so before quoting we check exactly where the leak is. If it turns out to be theirs, we tell you and help you report it.

    Age is the biggest cause. Galvanized rusts and copper pits. Shifting soil, tree roots, high water pressure and freeze-thaw cycles all add stress, plus accidental hits from fence posts, irrigation trenching and utility work. Poor original installation is another, such as pipe laid on sharp rock.

    We dig a pit at each end of the old line. A cable goes through the old pipe. A bursting head on the cable splits the old line outward, and new HDPE pipe follows right behind it into the same space. It only works when the old pipe is a fairly straight run, and most home service lines qualify.

    You can tighten an exposed fitting at a hose bib. The buried line is a different job. They need the leak located, 811 marking, safe digging around the gas and power lines nearby and code-approved fittings made up correctly. A mistake means a bigger leak and the same hole dug twice.

    Watch the bill first. A sudden jump with no change in use is the classic clue. Other signs include low pressure everywhere, soggy ground in dry weather, discolored water and a hissing sound near the meter. On a slab home, a warm spot on the floor can mean the leak is under the house.

    Type K copper, burial-rated PEX or HDPE poly. All three work well. Copper resists punctures and lasts decades, PEX flexes with the soil and HDPE is what we use for trenchless pulls. We recommend one after looking at your soil, line length and budget.
