    Pressure-Assist and Dual-Flush Repair in Maple Grove

    Pressure-assist toilets use a sealed vessel inside the china that compresses air with incoming water pressure, and the cartridge inside that vessel eventually wears out. You will not find most of these parts at a hardware store. We carry Flushmate cartridges and dual-flush seals for the common TOTO and American Standard models, and we check house pressure, since these units need steady line pressure to flush properly. Low line pressure gives you a weak, half-hearted flush.

    Toilet Tank and Bowl Crack Repair in Maple Grove

    Hairline cracks in a tank can sit for years and then split open when someone sets a heavy lid down or a bolt is tightened too far. Sealants and epoxy are a stopgap at best on a tank that holds water every day. We replace it. If the tank model is still made, a matching tank is often cheaper than a new toilet.

    Leaky Toilet Repair in Maple Grove

    Food coloring in the tank is the cheapest test we run. If color shows up in the bowl within fifteen minutes without a flush, water is leaking past the flapper or the flush valve seat, and the toilet is quietly refilling itself all day and night. A new flapper fixes most of these.

    Sensor and Flushometer Repair in Maple Grove

    Sensor flush valves fail in their own ways, from dead batteries and dirty sensor eyes to solenoids that stick open and keep a bowl running. We test the sensor before we replace anything. Batteries are the cheap fix. Ghost flushing on a sensor valve usually means the eye needs adjusting or cleaning.

    Toilet Flush Repair in Maple Grove, MN

    Two or three tries to get a full flush points to a flapper closing too early, a chain hooked in the wrong hole, scale in the rim jets, or a cracked overflow tube letting water bypass the bowl. Each one has a tell. Our plumber watches a complete flush with the tank lid off and replace the flush valve if the overflow tube is cracked, since it cannot be patched.

    Toilet Leaking Through the Ceiling in Maple Grove, MN

    Water coming through the ceiling under an upstairs bathroom usually traces back to a failed wax ring or a cracked supply nut, and the drywall below holds the evidence. Leave that toilet alone until we look. We pull the bowl, check the flange and reset it on a new seal, then we show you how far the water traveled so you can decide about the ceiling.

    Running Toilet Repair in Maple Grove
