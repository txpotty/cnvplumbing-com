    Grease, coffee grounds, pasta and rice pile up in the drain line. Hard-water scale gives that buildup something to cling to. Clearing the line and changing a few disposal habits usually solves it, unless the pipe has a belly or too little fall.

    Most take between two and four hours, including the sink, faucet, strainers and trap. Moving drains takes longer. A deeper bowl can mean lowering the trap and drain arm, which usually adds about an hour.

    Nearly all food service businesses must have one under local pretreatment rules. We size the trap or interceptor by your fixture flow and the local district's rules, then install it.

    Keep grease, coffee grounds, rice and pasta out of the drain. Run cold water during disposal use. Cold water keeps grease solid so the disposal can chop it up and push it through instead of letting it coat the pipe walls. A basket strainer in the other bowl helps too.

    Tightening a trap is easy enough to do yourself. Leave disposal wiring, dishwasher drains and grease traps to a pro, because errors there cause leaks and code problems.

    Restaurants typically clean every one to three months, though a busy fryer kitchen may need it every few weeks. Homes rarely need one. The common guideline is to clean before the grease and solids fill about a quarter of the trap's depth, and your local sewer district may set its own schedule.
