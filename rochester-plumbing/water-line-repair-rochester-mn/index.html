    Pipe Bursting and Pulling in Rochester

    Pipe bursting means splitting the old line in place while the new one follows behind it. It works best on straight runs. A cone-shaped head breaks the old pipe outward, and a length of HDPE trails behind it in the same path. You end up with a fresh line and two small pits, and the lawn, sidewalk and driveway in between stay where they are, which is the whole reason people pick this method over a trench.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Line Repair in Rochester

    Spot repairs on an accessible line usually run $350 to $1,500. The deeper the pipe, the higher the price. A trenched meter-to-house replacement is typically $1,800 to $5,500, while trenchless replacement tends to run $3,000 to $7,000 since it needs a boring rig or puller. The written price comes before we start.

    Type K copper, burial-rated PEX or HDPE poly. Each has its place. Copper resists punctures and lasts decades, PEX tolerates ground movement and freezing, and HDPE is the standard choice when we pull a line trenchless. We recommend one after looking at your soil, line length and budget.

    Most jurisdictions in Minnesota require a permit for a full service line replacement. Small repairs depend on the city. The permit usually comes from the city or county building department, and an inspector looks at the new pipe, the bedding and the burial depth before backfill. CNV Plumbing handles that paperwork for you.

    Scale buildup shortens water heater life and clogs aerators, which is why water softeners are so common in the state. Glacial clay and sandy soils are both common in Minnesota, and deep frost causes the ground to heave and settle each year. Tree roots wrap and crush shallow lines over time, while mineral scale builds up inside older pipe and thins the wall. Old galvanized is the top culprit.

    Keep trees and shrubs with big roots well away from the line. Know where the pipe runs. Frozen pipes are a constant winter concern in Minnesota, and deep frost can even reach buried water service lines during severe winters. A pressure gauge on a hose bib tells you if the regulator is failing, and anything over 80 psi is worth fixing before it starts wearing on the line. On galvanized lines, budgeting for a new line is cheaper than an emergency.

    In most places the water provider owns the meter and the pipe on the street side of it. You own the rest. Some providers draw the line at the curb stop instead, so we confirm where the leak sits before quoting anything. If it turns out to be theirs, we tell you and help you report it.

    Labor is warrantied, always. New pipe and fittings carry the manufacturer warranty as well, with the coverage listed on your quote before we begin. Keep the invoice. You will want it if anything comes up.

    Spot repairs usually take a few hours. A full replacement takes about a day. Jobs with a deep line, a lot of hardscape or a trenchless pull sometimes run two or three days when concrete has to be cut and patched or the inspector cannot come right away. We give you a time estimate with the quote.
