    Home Plumbing Repairs We Handle in Rochester, MN

    Repiping and Supply Line Replacement in Rochester

    Polybutylene, the gray plastic pipe used for years in home construction, fails from the inside without warning. It gets replaced with PEX or copper, same as rusted galvanized and pinholed copper, routing new lines through the attic and walls so the slab stays intact and the house keeps water each night. We patch the access holes, ready for paint.

    Pipe Repair and Repipes in Rochester, MN

    Pinhole leaks and rust-colored water from the tap usually point to supply pipe near the end of its working life in an older house. We patch single leaks with a copper repair or PEX transition. More than one leak usually means repiping. A pipe split by a hard freeze gets cut out and replaced, then pressure-tested.

    Slab Leak Detection and Repair in Rochester

    Slab leaks hide under concrete until something gives them away. Freeze and thaw movement can crack older sewer laterals, and tree roots often find their way into the joints. We locate them with acoustic listening gear and a thermal camera, so the only concrete we break is the small patch right over the leak, not half the kitchen floor. After that, you choose the repair. Some leaks get a spot repair, others a reroute overhead that skips the slab entirely.

    Drain Cleaning and Sewer Camera Service in Rochester

    Wipes are not flushable, whatever the package says. Kitchen grease, hair and wipes cause most slow drains, and for a main line backup we cable it open, then run a camera to look for roots, offset joints or a belly where water sits in the pipe. We send you the video file.

    Water Heater Repair and Installs in Rochester

    A water heater works hardest in winter, when incoming water is coldest. We work on gas, electric, tank and tankless heaters from Rheem and Bradford White, and the usual culprits are a heavy layer of sediment or a thermocouple that has quit reading the pilot. A cold shower is often a twenty-minute part swap. Replacements go in with an expansion tank and drain pan where code calls for them.

    Emergency Home Plumbing Help in Rochester

    A gas smell or sewage backup cannot wait until Monday. Our trucks carry enough parts to stop most emergencies on the first visit, and while the plumber is driving over, the dispatcher walks you through shutting off the water main or the gas valve at the meter. Fast shutoff saves floors and cabinets.

    Faucet and Fixture Repair in Rochester

    Most dripping faucets need a cartridge, not a new faucet. The truck has cartridges for the big brands, Moen and Delta included, and when you do want new fixtures we install them, connect new braided supply lines, and check the angle stops under the sink while we are there. Old stops fail when touched.

    Pressure Regulators and Shutoff Valves in Rochester, MN

    Water pressure over 80 psi wears out hoses and valves early. A gauge on the hose bib tells us your static pressure in a minute, and if it reads high we install or replace a pressure reducing valve and set it in the 50 to 70 psi range most fixtures are rated for. We also replace main shutoffs that will not turn.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Residential Plumbing Service in Rochester

    Pricing depends on the job. Simple repairs like a faucet cartridge or toilet rebuild tend to cost $150 to $350, a water heater installation is often $1,200 to $3,500, and slab leak repairs or full repipes can reach several thousand dollars. You get an exact quote after we diagnose.

    Once a year is a good rule. In homes with hard water, sediment builds faster and the tank may need flushing twice a year, and a yearly check lets us test the relief valve, look at the anode rod and catch a weeping fitting before it rusts through the jacket. Anodes usually last three to five years.

    Many do, especially anything that changes the system. New installations and gas line work generally need a permit from the Rochester building department, and we know which jobs trigger one and which do not. We handle the paperwork and schedule the inspection.

    Hard water carries dissolved calcium and magnesium. Soft water has had most of those minerals removed, usually by an ion-exchange softener, and the difference shows up as scale in water heaters, white crust on faucets, spotty dishes and soap that will not lather well. Fixtures last longer on soft water.

    Most repairs take one to three hours. A leaky pipe or clogged drain is often done in under two, while water heater installs take most of a day and full repipes can stretch over several days depending on the size of the house and access. We give you a timeline up front.

    Emergencies go to the top of the board. We aim to have a plumber at the house within a few hours, and for a burst pipe or sewage backup we will walk you through closing the main shutoff on the phone so the damage stops while we drive. Grab towels and a bucket while you wait.

    Soft water and normal pressure prevent a lot of what we see. Grease out of the drains and a yearly pressure test help too, since anything over 80 psi wears out supply hoses and fixtures faster than people expect. An annual check finds small issues early.

    Swapping a showerhead or a toilet flapper is fine for most people. Gas lines, water heaters and anything behind a wall belong with a licensed plumber, for safety and because those jobs usually need a permit and an inspection that a homeowner repair can fail. Plumber's tape will not fix a gas leak.
