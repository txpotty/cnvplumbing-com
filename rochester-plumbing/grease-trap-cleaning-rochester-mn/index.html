    Hydro-Jetting for Grease Lines in Rochester, MN

    A cable machine pokes a hole through grease. Jetting actually removes it. Our jetters run around 3,000 to 4,000 psi through a spinning nozzle, which scours the whole inside of the pipe and pushes the loosened grease downstream. Afterward a camera pass confirms the pipe is clean.

    Indoor Grease Trap Cleaning in Rochester

    Indoor traps usually sit under the three-compartment sink or in a recessed pit near the dish station. Room to work is tight. Mats go down first, the contents get pumped or scooped into sealed containers, and the floor gets wiped so prep can start right after. We also check the gasket and the flow control.

    Emergency Grease Trap Cleaning in Rochester, MN

    Sometimes the trap is fine and the problem is downstream. A camera tells us quickly. A trap that is half empty while floor sinks keep backing up points to a clog in the lateral toward the main, which needs jetting, not another pump-out. We can jet on the same visit.

    Scheduled Grease Trap Maintenance Plans in Rochester

    Most pretreatment programs use the 25 percent rule: pump before floating grease and settled solids together take up a quarter of the trap's liquid depth. Some cities set a fixed interval instead, often 90 days for interceptors. Whichever rule your program uses, we schedule to it. Changes are easy.

    Large Outdoor Grease Interceptor Cleaning in Rochester

    Gravity interceptors in the parking lot often hold 750 to 2,000 gallons across two or three compartments. We pump each compartment on its own. A hard grease cap gets broken up with a pressure wand before pumping, otherwise it plugs the suction hose and half of it stays in the tank. No one goes inside without confined-space gear.
