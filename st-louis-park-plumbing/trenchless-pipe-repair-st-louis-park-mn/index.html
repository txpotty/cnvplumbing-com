    Frequently Asked Questions About Trenchless Pipe Repair in St. Louis Park

    Most residential trenchless jobs run $80 to $250 per foot. Length drives it. Pipe diameter, the type of damage and whether we line or burst all move the price, so a camera inspection comes first and the written estimate from we follow from what the footage shows.

    An epoxy liner, correctly installed, is rated to last 50 years or more. HDPE pipe used in bursting has a similar long service life.

    Lining reduces the inside diameter slightly, but the smooth surface usually improves flow. Pipe bursting can maintain or increase the diameter.

    Compared to digging, yes. Far less dirt is disturbed. Trenchless repair leaves trees, plants and soil in place, and because the old pipe usually stays in the ground as the host, very little material ends up hauled to a landfill.

    Drains that clog again a few weeks after clearing. Backups are a big sign. Sewer odors, soggy yard spots and unusually green strips of lawn often mean a broken line below.

    Lining builds a new pipe inside the old one. Bursting replaces it. With CIPP, a resin-soaked liner cures against the old pipe wall, while bursting breaks the old pipe apart and pulls new HDPE into its place, leaving a completely new pipe.

    CIPP lining jobs usually take one to three days. Bursting can run longer. Pipe bursting may take slightly more, depending on length and access, but either one is much faster than open trenching, which can stretch to weeks once excavation and restoration are counted.

    We use liners and resins from established manufacturers such as Perma-Liner and similar systems rated for sewer use, picked to match the pipe size and condition.
