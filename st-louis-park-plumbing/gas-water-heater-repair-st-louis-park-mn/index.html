    Pilot Light and Ignition Service in St. Louis Park

    No hot water and a dark pilot window usually point to the ignition side of the heater. The tech pulls the pilot assembly and checks the thermocouple tip, the orifice and the igniter wire. A thermocouple that tests weak on the millivolt meter will not hold the gas valve's magnet open, so the pilot drops out a minute after you relight it by the label. Common pilot kits ride on most of our trucks.

    Flammable Vapor Sensor Repair in St. Louis Park

    Since about 2003, most residential gas tanks have been built with a flammable vapor ignition resistant combustion chamber. A flame arrestor screen at the base lets air in and keeps flame from getting out. When lint, dust or pet hair coats that screen, the burner starves, the chamber overheats and a thermal switch shuts the whole heater down, sometimes several times a week. The screen and the chamber both get cleaned. A tripped thermal switch gets replaced.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Gas Water Heater Repair in St. Louis Park

    Most gas water heater repairs in St. Louis Park, MN land between $150 and $800. Thermocouple and pilot work is the cheap end, roughly $150 to $350. A gas control valve replacement usually runs $400 to $800 or more once you add the part, the labor and the leak testing on every joint we disturb. We quote once we know the cause.

    A weak thermocouple is the usual cause. A plugged pilot orifice can also make the flame too small to heat it. On vapor-resistant tanks, a lint-clogged flame arrestor can starve the burner and trip the thermal switch, which looks like a pilot outage from the outside. Drafts can blow out older pilots.

    Most repairs take one to three hours. Pilot and thermocouple jobs usually wrap up in under an hour. A gas control valve, a power vent blower or a flue rebuild takes longer, and if the part has to be ordered from a supply house the repair can stretch to a second visit a day or two later. We tell you the timeline before we start.

    Eight to twelve years is typical for a gas tank. Tanks that get flushed and get a new anode can run past that. A heater near the end of that range that needs a gas valve, a flue repair and a relief valve all at once is usually a better candidate for replacement than for another round of parts. Check the serial number for the build date.

    Lukewarm water, a pilot that keeps going out and a rumbling noise from the bottom of the tank are the usual early signs of trouble. Rusty hot water or a puddle at the base can mean the tank is on its way out. A rotten egg smell is different. Leave the house and treat it as a leak until it is tested.

    Part replacement typically does not. A thermocouple or gas valve swap counts as a repair almost everywhere. Replacing the water heater, relocating it or changing the gas piping or venting generally does need a permit, and we pull it, book the inspection and handle the paperwork with the local building office. Permits add a few days at most.

    Same-day repair is the goal on most calls. Thermocouples, pilot assemblies and thermostats are stocked, so those jobs rarely need a return trip. When a heater needs a brand-specific gas valve or a power vent blower, the supply house may need a day to get it, and we will leave the heater safe in the meantime. We give you a time for the return visit.

    Relighting a pilot by the label is fine. Swapping gas valves, thermocouples or vent sections should be left to a licensed plumber. A bad gas joint or a vent that is put back slightly wrong can leak fuel or carbon monoxide into the house, and many manufacturer warranties are void after unlicensed gas work. Codes in St. Louis Park, MN generally require a licensed plumber for gas work.
