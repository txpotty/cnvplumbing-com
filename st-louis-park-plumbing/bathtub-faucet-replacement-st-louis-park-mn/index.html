    Frequently Asked Questions About Bathtub Faucet Replacement in St. Louis Park

    Most tub faucet replacements run $250 to $600 with a standard faucet. A trim and cartridge swap on a valve that stays in the wall sits near the low end, while replacing the valve itself, especially behind tile with no access panel, lands near the top. The price is set before work starts.

    The diverter is wearing out. On a spout diverter the rubber gate inside no longer seals, and on a three-handle faucet the middle stem washer has worn, so water splits between the spout and the shower head instead of going all to one. Spout diverters are cheap to replace.

    Replace cartridges before they fail badly. A slight drip or a handle that starts to stiffen is the early warning, and swapping the cartridge then costs far less than waiting until the brass seat is cut or the valve body is pitted. Early repairs stay cheap.

    If it is the same brand and series, often yes. Same-series trim bolts onto the existing valve, but mixed brands rarely fit. Find the model number before buying.

    A worn cartridge or stem washer is the usual cause. On a single-handle valve the seals inside the cartridge have hardened or cracked, and on a two- or three-handle faucet the washer and the brass seat under it are worn, so water slips past even with the handle closed. New parts stop it.

    Our trucks carry cartridges for the brands we see most. We stock common Moen, Delta, Kohler and Pfister cartridges plus universal stems and seats. Odd valves take a day.

    Plan on three to five hours. We open the wall, swap the valve, pressure-test it and then install the trim and cover. Any tile patch takes longer.

    Water across much of Minnesota is hard, and many supplies also carry iron or manganese that stain fixtures and laundry. Calcium and magnesium build up on cartridge seals and inside the spout diverter. It stiffens handles and wears seals early, leading to drips and a stuck diverter.
