    Repair vs. Replace Your Water Line in St. Louis Park, MN

    One leak on a newer copper or PEX line is a spot repair. Repeated leaks on galvanized pipe call for a new line. Age matters too, since most service lines past 40 years old are living on borrowed time. Spot repairs often run a few hundred dollars while a full replacement costs more and resets the clock for decades. You see the cut-out section yourself.

    PEX Water Line Repair in St. Louis Park, MN

    PEX itself rarely fails underground, but the joints and the first few feet near the house take a beating. Rats and gophers sometimes chew it. A shovel or a fence post auger can nick it, and pipe left in the sun before it was buried can get brittle. We cut out the damaged section and install new pipe with crimp, clamp or expansion fittings, matched to the PEX brand already in the ground.

    Pressure Regulator and Shutoff Repair in St. Louis Park

    High static pressure shortens the life of the service line and every fixture behind it. The usual code limit is 80 psi. We gauge the pressure at a hose bib, and if it reads high we replace the pressure reducing valve with a Watts or Zurn Wilkins unit. Old gate valves that will not close get replaced with full-port ball valves. So the shutoff works when you need it.

    Galvanized Water Line Replacement in St. Louis Park, MN

    Galvanized service lines were common in houses built before the 1970s, and a lot of them are still in the ground. Private wells are widespread, so homeowners around St. Louis Park should test for hardness and iron before choosing a treatment system. Scale builds up inside and the opening shrinks. Rusty water at the first tap in the morning is a classic sign, along with pressure that drops when two fixtures run at once. Replacing the full run with copper or PEX clears all of it up. Most homes are done in a day.

    Galvanized Pipe Water Line Repair in St. Louis Park, MN

    Galvanized steel service lines rust from the inside out. Once one spot leaks, the pipe on either side is usually just as thin. We can clamp or replace a short section to get you through, which can hold for a while if the pipe is not too far gone. More often we recommend replacing the whole run from the meter to the house with copper or PEX. That fixes the pressure too.

    PEX and Copper Water Line Repair in St. Louis Park

    Copper and PEX fail in different places. Copper tends to pinhole where a rock rubs it or where the water chemistry eats the wall from inside. PEX usually fails at a fitting. We cut out the bad section of copper and sweat or press in a new length, and for PEX we use the matching crimp or expansion tool so the joint is made the way the manufacturer intends.

The crew pumps the hole, finds the break and decides whether a coupling will do or whether a section needs replacing, setting up a temporary supply to the house if the job will take a while. Most breaks are fixed the same day. We photograph the damage for your insurance or HOA.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Line Repair in St. Louis Park

    Spot repairs on an accessible line usually run $350 to $1,500. Depth drives most of that. A trenched meter-to-house replacement is typically $1,800 to $5,500, while trenchless replacement tends to run $3,000 to $7,000 since it needs a boring rig or puller. The written price comes before we start.

    We back our workmanship. New pipe and fittings carry the manufacturer warranty as well, with the coverage listed on your quote before we begin. Keep the invoice. You will want it if anything comes up.

    Look for a wet or extra-green patch in the yard. A hiss at the meter is a giveaway. Low pressure at every fixture, a bill that keeps rising and rusty or cloudy water can all point to a damaged line. The surest check is your meter. A moving dial means water is escaping somewhere.

    Two to four hours for most spot repairs. A full replacement is typically a one-day job. Trenchless jobs are often a single day too, even with a few hours spent setting the pits and the rig. In most cases your water is back on before evening.

    Small spot repairs often do not need one. Full replacements usually do. Rules vary by jurisdiction in St. Louis Park, so CNV Plumbing checks the local requirement for your address and pulls the permit when one applies. An inspection happens with the pipe still exposed.

    Age is the biggest cause. Old pipe corrodes. Shifting soil, tree roots, high water pressure and freeze-thaw cycles all add stress, and an old line gets damaged by excavation work more often than most people realize. Poor original installation is another, like rocks dumped right on the pipe.

    Most jurisdictions in Minnesota require a permit for a full service line replacement. Spot repairs vary. The permit usually comes from the city or county building department, with an inspection of the exposed pipe and bedding before we cover it. CNV Plumbing handles that paperwork for you.

    Often the pipe itself is not covered. Damage it causes may be. Some homeowners carry separate service line coverage through their insurer or the water provider, which can pay for the repair or replacement of the buried line itself. We provide photos and an itemized invoice for your claim.
