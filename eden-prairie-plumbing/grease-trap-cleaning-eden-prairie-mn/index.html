    Commercial Grease Trap Cleaning in Eden Prairie

    Restaurants and hospital kitchens in Eden Prairie run the same basic setup, a trap or interceptor sitting between the sinks and the sewer, sized by flow rate or by gallons.     Large Outdoor Grease Interceptor Cleaning in Eden Prairie

    Gravity interceptors in the parking lot often hold 750 to 2,000 gallons across two or three compartments. Each compartment gets pumped separately. A hard grease cap gets broken up with a pressure wand before pumping, otherwise it plugs the suction hose and half of it stays in the tank. No one goes inside without confined-space gear.

    Residential Grease Trap Cleaning in Eden Prairie, MN

    Homes rarely have a grease trap, though a few large custom kitchens and some home-based catering setups in Eden Prairie, MN put a small unit under the sink. Most are 10 to 20 gallon boxes.     Indoor Grease Trap Cleaning in Eden Prairie

    Indoor traps usually sit under the three-compartment sink or in a recessed pit near the dish station. Room to work is tight. Mats go down first, the contents get pumped or scooped into sealed containers, and the floor gets wiped so prep can start right after. Gasket and flow control get checked too.

    Grease Trap Hydro-Jetting in Eden Prairie, MN

    Some traps have years of grease baked onto the walls and inlet piping. Pumping alone leaves that crust in place. Jetting the empty tank and its inlet line strips it back to bare walls and gets back the capacity that crust was taking. Plan on about an extra hour.

    Grease Trap Inspection and Repair in Eden Prairie

    An empty trap is the only time anyone can see what is wrong with it. Cracked baffles and missing outlet tees are the usual finds. A missing outlet tee lets the floating grease cap pour straight into the sewer, and it is one of the most common failures we find on older concrete interceptors. We can often fix small problems on the spot.

    Emergency Grease Trap Services in Eden Prairie, MN

    Gray water coming up through the floor sinks in the middle of service is the classic sign the trap has hit capacity. We send a truck the same day when we can. The crew pumps the trap first, then checks whether the line past the outlet is also packed with grease, because pumping alone will not fix a plugged lateral. Sometimes it needs jetting too.
