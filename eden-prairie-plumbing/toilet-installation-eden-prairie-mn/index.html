    Swapping an old 3.5 gallon toilet for a modern one is the easiest water savings in the house. The old unit comes out in about twenty minutes.     Low-Flow WaterSense Toilet Installation in Eden Prairie, MN

    WaterSense toilets are capped at 1.28 gallons per flush, and some dual-flush models drop to around 0.8 for liquid waste. It adds up quickly. Replacing a single 3.5 gallon toilet in a household of four can save several thousand gallons a year, and some water utilities have offered rebates on high-efficiency models, so it is worth asking yours.     High-Efficiency Toilet Installation in Eden Prairie, MN

    High-efficiency toilets flush on 1.28 gallons or less, against 3.5 gallons or more for toilets from before the early 1990s. Check for the WaterSense label. We steer customers toward models with a MaP score of 800 grams or higher, because a cheap low-flow bowl that needs two flushes saves nothing. Dual-flush models add a lighter half flush.

    Wall-Hung Carrier Systems in Eden Prairie

    In-wall carriers from Geberit and Grohe each mount a little differently, and the instructions matter. We follow the maker's frame height chart exactly. A frame set wrong puts the bowl too low or too high, and once tile is up there is no adjusting it. Measure twice here.

    Watch the base and the tank. Water that reappears on the floor after you wipe it up points to a cracked bowl or a failed seal. We rule out the seal first because it is cheaper, but cracked porcelain cannot be fixed for good, so a crack means a new toilet.

    It is a toilet with the seat about 17 to 19 inches off the floor, roughly chair height, compared with 15 inches or so on a standard bowl. Most adults find it easier on the knees. Children and shorter people may find their feet dangle, so for a family bath a standard height with a grab bar can be the better call.

    Water across much of Minnesota is hard, and many supplies also carry iron or manganese that discolor fixtures and laundry. Hard water leaves scale in the rim jets and on the fill valve over time. Flush power drops as jets clog. Look for full glazing and a simple fill valve, and pour vinegar down the overflow tube now and then to keep the jets clear.

    Figure one to two hours for a standard floor-mount toilet. Some jobs run a full day. Wall-hung systems and drain relocations on a slab mean opening walls or floors, so those get scheduled as their own project with time for patches to cure.

    A one-piece toilet is cast as a single unit, so there is no tank-to-bowl joint to leak. Expect a higher price and more weight. A two-piece bolts the tank to the bowl with a rubber gasket, which makes it lighter to carry up stairs and cheaper to fix, since you can replace just the tank or just the bowl if one cracks. Both flush well.

    Homeowners set basic toilets all the time. The trouble spots are flange height and bolt torque.
