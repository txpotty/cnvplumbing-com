    Trenchless Sewer Line Repair in Brooklyn Park, MN

    Trenchless repair means we fix the pipe without opening a trench along its full length. Two access holes, sometimes one. Pipe bursting pulls a new HDPE line through the old one and splits the old pipe outward, while cured-in-place lining pushes a resin-soaked felt tube into the old pipe and hardens it into a new wall. Both save driveways and mature trees. Not every line qualifies, and we will say so.

    Sewer Vent Pipe Repair in Brooklyn Park, MN

    A plugged vent makes drains gurgle and traps run dry. Sewer gas follows soon after. Bird nests, leaves and ice can block a roof vent, and older galvanized or cast iron vent stacks crack inside the walls where nobody sees them, which is why we look at the vent whenever the lateral checks out fine. Fixes range from a quick clearing to a new section of stack.

    Sewer Camera Inspection in Brooklyn Park

    Every repair starts with a look inside. The camera goes in at the nearest cleanout.     Sewer Pipe Relining in Brooklyn Park, MN

    Relining builds a new pipe inside the old one. The old pipe gets jetted clean before anything else. A flexible liner saturated with epoxy is pulled or inverted into place, inflated against the old wall and left to cure, so every crack and leaking joint is covered by one jointless pipe. The finished pipe loses a little diameter. On a four-inch residential line, that rarely matters.

    Sewer Line Backups and Blockages in Brooklyn Park

    Water coming up in the tub when the washer drains is a classic main line symptom. The clog is past the branches.     Bellied Sewer Line Correction in Brooklyn Park, MN

    A belly is a low spot where the pipe has sagged and holds standing water. Paper and solids settle there. Freeze and thaw movement can crack older sewer laterals, and tree roots often find their way into the joints. We measure the depth of standing water on camera, dig out that section, re-bed the pipe on compacted gravel and set it back to a quarter inch of fall per foot, which is the typical code minimum for a four-inch line. Not every sag needs digging, and we will tell you which yours is.
