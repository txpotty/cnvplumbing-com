    Slab Leaks From Ground Movement in Bloomington

    Pipe set in concrete cannot flex when the ground moves, so it cracks at elbows and where it rises through the slab.     Frequently Asked Questions About Leak Detection in Bloomington

    Most leak detection in Bloomington, MN runs $250 to $800, depending on where the leak is and how many lines we have to test. Slab leaks cost more. You get a written estimate after the first assessment.

    Some leaks you can see, like a dripping faucet. Hidden leaks take equipment. Slab and wall leaks require acoustic gear, thermal cameras or tracer gas, and guessing usually means opening the wrong wall.

    One to three hours covers most homes, though a big house with a long yard line or a pool can run past that. Commercial jobs run longer. We give you a time estimate when we book.

    Shut every fixture off and watch the meter. A moving dial or spinning leak indicator means water is going somewhere. So is a sudden jump in your bill.

    Pipe material matters more than brand. Galvanized rusts out at threaded joints. Old polybutylene fails early, and even copper or PEX can leak from bad installation, too much pressure or foundation movement.

    Galvanized steel and polybutylene top the list in older homes, since galvanized corrodes at the threads and polybutylene breaks down from the inside. Old copper gets pinholes. PVC and PEX hold up better but can still leak at fittings or from installation mistakes.

    Acoustic ground microphones, thermal cameras, line tracers, moisture meters and pressure gauges go on the truck for every leak call we run. Every tool confirms a different leak type. Results are more reliable when tools agree.

    Watch for a water bill that jumps without a change in use, damp spots on walls or floors, a musty smell, or water running with every fixture off. A moving meter is the clearest sign. Turn off every fixture and watch it for ten minutes.
