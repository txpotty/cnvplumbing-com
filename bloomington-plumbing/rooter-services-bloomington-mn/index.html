    Frequently Asked Questions About Rooter Services in Bloomington

    A single clogged sink or tub in Bloomington, MN usually runs $200 to $350. Main sewer lines with roots or jetting tend to land between $400 and $1,000. Location changes the price. A clean outdoor cleanout keeps the price down, while pulling a toilet or working through a roof vent adds time. You get the number before we start.

    Rooting uses a spinning cable with cutting blades to break up clogs and roots. Jetting is different. Hydro jetting uses high-pressure water to scrub the pipe walls clean. Tough lines often get both, rooting to open the path and jetting to clean out what remains.

    Snaking runs a spinning cable with a cutting head through the clog. Hydro jetting sends water at several thousand psi through a nozzle that scrubs the whole pipe wall as it is pulled back toward the cleanout. Jetting cleans more. Snaking handles one-time clogs, while jetting is for grease lines and scaled mains that keep coming back.

    Plan on 1 to 3 hours for a single clogged drain. Heavy main line root cutting or a long jetting run can take 3 to 6 hours. You get a time estimate first. Once the tech sees the cleanout and asks which fixtures are slow, they can give you a closer estimate of how long it will run.

    No permit is required for routine rooting. Installing a new cleanout, digging up a broken section or replacing the sewer lateral usually does require a plumbing permit and an inspection. Permits for repair work are on us.

    Most rooter jobs include a limited guarantee for a set number of days. A repeat clog in that window means a free return visit.

    We run RIDGID, Spartan and Gorlitz augers, plus sewer cameras and jetters from the same tier of commercial makers. These machines take daily abuse and keep working. Brand matters less than setup. Even a top machine can hurt a pipe with the wrong head, so picking the cutter gets as much attention as the machine.

    Our trucks carry Spartan and RIDGID machines, plus jetters sized for residential and commercial lines. Both brands hold up to daily use. Several sizes of cable and cutter ride on each truck, so the tech matches the tool to the pipe rather than using one setup everywhere.
