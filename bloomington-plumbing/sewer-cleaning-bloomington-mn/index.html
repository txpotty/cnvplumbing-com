    Root Removal From Sewer Lines in Bloomington

    Trees send roots toward any moisture they can find. A sewer joint is a steady source. We use a sectional cable with a root saw sized to the pipe to cut the mass free and pull it out, then follow with a jetter if fine root hairs are left behind, and a camera shows where roots are entering so you can choose between lining and repair.

    Video Inspection Before Cleaning in Bloomington, MN

    Some lines need a look first. Old pipe especially. Seeing the pipe first lets us pick the cheapest method that will work, and avoid damaging a line that is already failing.

    Sewer Camera Inspections in Bloomington, MN

    On a repeat backup, the camera goes in first. Guessing costs money. A waterproof color camera on a push rod shows the inside of the line live on a screen, so we can see the clog, measure its distance from the cleanout and spot any crack or sag.

    Grease and Scale Buildup Cleaning in Bloomington, MN

    Grease bonds with mineral scale into a hard crust. Scale buildup shortens water heater life and clogs showerheads, which is why water softeners are so common in the state. Over years the opening shrinks. A cable can punch a hole through the crust, but a jetter cleans the walls back to full size and leaves less for new grease to grab onto, and the method depends on pipe material and condition.

    Root Masses in Bloomington Sewer Laterals

    Mature trees and old clay laterals do not mix. Roots find the joints. We cut roots in several passes, then look on camera to judge the damage and talk about lining or a spot repair.

    Sewer Cleaning Versus Sewer Repair in Bloomington, MN
